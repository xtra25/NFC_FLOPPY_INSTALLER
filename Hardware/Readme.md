# Fake 3.5" Floppy Disk Drive

A Commodore 1541-style prop with a **working mechanical eject mechanism** and a
hidden NFC reader bay.

Insert a disk and a lever pushes the front button out. Press the button and the
disk is ejected. There are no springs and no electronics in the mechanism — it
is a first-class lever driven by the disk itself.

It takes both 3D-printed dummy disks and real 3.5" floppies, which is most of
where the design difficulty lives: a real floppy is 3.3 mm thick and a printed
one 3.6, and the retention has to work with both.

Everything is generated by one Blender script. Every dimension is derived from
the ones above it, so changing the disk thickness or the recess depth
propagates through the whole model rather than leaving something behind.

## How it works

```
        disk in  ──►  bar pushed back 11.0 mm
                          │
                      lever pivots ±17.79°
                          │
        button out ◄──  4.278 mm  (= 11.0 × 7/18)
```

The pivot sits **between** the two ends, 7 mm from the button and 18 mm from
the bar. That is what inverts the motion — put it at either end and pressing
the button would push the disk further in. The 18:7 split is also the
reduction: it is why 11 mm of disk travel becomes 4.3 mm of button travel
instead of 11.

Both ends of the lever are round bosses gripped by two-walled forks, not pins
in slots. A pin was tried; it was a 2.4 mm stub loaded in bending with the
print layers running across it, and it broke on the second press.

Two compliant tongues in the channel floor hold the disk. The bump does not
push the tongue down — it lifts the *disk*, until the disk meets the channel
roof, and only the remainder bends the tongue. So retention is governed by
`bump height − vertical clearance`, which is why the channel is kept tight and
the bump is tall. With a generous channel a real disk simply floats over the
bump and is not retained at all.

| `Guide_Dowel` | 2 | ⌀4 × 10 mm — **print lying down** |
| `NFC_Cover` | 1 | |
| `Gland_Lower` / `Gland_Upper` | 1 each | two-piece cable gland |
| `Retention_Coupon` | 1 | test piece, not part of the drive |
| `Reference_Disk` | — | clearance check only, do not print |

The middle plate is what makes the rest possible. Before it existed the bays
ran all the way down to the disk channel and the moving parts had nothing
underneath, so they sagged into it; a floor was impossible because everything
is fitted from below. Being a separate plate sandwiched between the two shells
resolves that, and the same four screws clamp all three.

## Hardware

| | Qty | Where |
|---|---|---|
| M3 × 30 countersunk self-tapping | 4 | base + plate + top |
| M3 × 8 countersunk self-tapping | 4 | NFC cover |

**Self-tapping, not machine screws.** The pilot holes are 2.1 mm, sized for a
~2.0 mm core. A machine screw wants 2.6 mm and would strip these.

The lengths are not approximate:

- **M3 × 25 will not reach.** It has to clear 23.6 mm of base and plate before
  it bites; a 25 would engage 1.4 mm. The 30 engages 6.4 mm, which is the
  2 × diameter rule for plastic.
- **M3 × 10 is too long for the NFC cover.** Its pilot hole ends at Z = 10.3
  and a 10 would reach 11.0 — it bottoms out and lifts the cover instead of
  clamping it.

The countersinks are cut for a **5.5 mm head at 90°**. A different head needs
`M3_HEAD_D` changed; every countersink follows it.

`Lever_Rod` and `Guide_Dowel` can equally be lengths of 5 mm and 4 mm steel
rod, which is stronger than any printed version.

## Printing

PLA+ throughout. Nothing needs supports except the mechanism bay ceiling.

**Print `Retention_Coupon` first.** It is about 7 g and ten minutes. It
reproduces the disk channel and one retention tongue at full size, with the
channel roof at the real height — which is the part that matters, since
retention depends on the disk's vertical clearance. Slide a disk in from the
low-Y end until it clicks, and try it with both a real and a printed disk.

Print `Lever_Rod` and `Guide_Dowel` **lying down**. Standing up, the layer
lines run across the load and they snap. This is not a detail: the pivot in an
earlier version was moulded into the shell, printed upright, and broke at about
25 N of finger force.

### Tuning retention

One knob, and it is the only linear one — thickness and length are both cubed,
so their smallest useful step is already a 20–40 % jump:

| Change | Effect |
|---|---|
| `RET_W` +0.3 mm | +5 % retention |
| `RET_T` +0.1 mm | +42 % |
| `RET_LEN` −1 mm | +21 % |

As shipped: about 1.2 N holding a real disk and 2.1 N a printed one, needing
3.1 N and 5.4 N at the button to eject. `RET_W` can go up to 7.0 before the
right-hand tongue gets within half a millimetre of the NFC bay wall.

## Assembly

1. `Drive_Top` upside down, bay facing up.
2. Drop in `Front_Button` (cap through the bezel slot) and `Push_Bar`.
3. Drop in `Eject_Lever`, both bosses inside the forks.
4. Push `Lever_Rod` down through the lever into its blind socket.
5. Press the two `Guide_Dowel` into their blind holes.
6. Lay `Mech_Plate` on — its tower captures the lower end of the rod.
7. `Drive_Base` on top, four M3 × 30.
8. NFC module and `NFC_Cover` last, four M3 × 8.

The rod is press fit in the top shell and sliding everywhere else, which is
why it goes in at step 4 and not later.

## Running the script

Blender 3.0 or newer. Open the Scripting tab, load
`fake_floppy_drive.py`, Run. It wipes the scene and rebuilds every part, so it
is safe to re-run after editing any dimension.

To check a part came out in one piece rather than as two overlapping shells —
the failure mode that costs the most time here — run in the console:

```python
count_shells(bpy.data.objects["Push_Bar"])   # should return 1
```

## Reference disk

The model is dimensioned against a printed dummy disk (90.0 × 93.9 × 3.6 mm)
whose back-face pockets the retention bumps engage. `Reference_Disk` in the
script is a plain block for checking clearances, **not** a printable disk.

it is not covered by this repository's.
